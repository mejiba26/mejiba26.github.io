Disclaimer Not investment advice, not a recommendation, and not a current price target. The price date is October 30, 2024.

Summary

The question the model was built to answer is a narrow one: what is RTX actually worth per share? The free cash flow model discounts four years of projected cash flow and a terminal value at a 6.15% weighted average cost of capital, then bridges from enterprise value to equity value with net debt. That produces $128.16 per share against a price of $122.14 on October 30, 2024, an implied upside of about 5%.

Two independent cross checks bracket that figure. Applying the peer median EV/EBITDA of 17.05 times implies $133.10 per share. Applying the peer median P/E of 22.63 times to management's adjusted EPS guidance midpoint implies $125.37 per share. The three methods give a range of $125 to $133, which is why the conclusion is stated as a range rather than a point.

On profitability RTX led the peer set it was benchmarked against. Its 19.21% gross margin compared with 12.22% for Lockheed Martin and 3.13% for Boeing. On liquidity it trailed both, with a current ratio of 0.99 and a quick ratio of 0.60.

Discount rate

Because the model values the whole firm rather than the equity claim alone, the discount rate is the weighted average cost of capital rather than the cost of equity. The cost of equity comes from CAPM. RTX's low beta reflects a defensive, low volatility profile backed by steady government contracts.

In 2026 I audited the original Fall 2024 model with the help of an AI assistant and rebuilt the valuation. Every change is listed so a reader can see exactly what moved and why. The two that did most of the work are the first two.

  1. Discount rate

    The original entered a 5.5% expected market return, which produced a required return of 4.94% and an implied equity risk premium of only 1.22%. That cost of equity was then used to discount free cash flow to the whole firm. The revision uses the Damodaran implied equity risk premium of 4.29% for a cost of equity of 6.60%, blended with a 4.41% after tax cost of debt into a WACC of 6.15%.

  2. Equity bridge

    The original subtracted total liabilities of about $102 billion from enterprise value. The revision subtracts net debt of $35.47 billion, since operating liabilities are already reflected in free cash flow.

  3. Terminal value

    The original reported a year four value of $381.02 billion without showing the calculation. The revision shows the Gordon growth formula with a 3.0% terminal growth rate, which sits below the 6.15% WACC.

  4. Growth assumptions

    The original text described a single 5% growth rate while the model itself used 7%, 12%, 14% and 12% for 2025 through 2028. The text now matches the model.

  5. Share count

    Updated from 1,333,200,000 to 1,331,016,397 shares, per the 10-Q cover page as of September 30, 2024.

  6. P/E ratio

    The body cited a 2023 P/E of 37.06 while Appendix A showed 34.98. Both were replaced with a trailing twelve month P/E of 34.50.

  7. Income statement

    Operating expenses, EBIT and earnings before taxes were restated. The interest expense line was replaced with net non operating expense. Noncontrolling interest was broken out so net income ties to earnings per share. The 2024 gross profit entry was corrected to 15,184, and the decline in shares outstanding was corrected from 6.5% to 6.7%.

  8. Peer data

    Boeing's gross profit margin was corrected from 3.62% to 3.13%.

  9. Relative valuation

    Appendix E is new. It compares RTX with Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics and Northrop Grumman on EV/EBITDA and P/E, and it is the source of the two cross checks in the valuation summary above.

The case against

Stated plainly rather than only the bull case. The valuation is sensitive to near term growth assumptions that may not materialize if commercial aerospace deliveries or defense budgets disappoint. RTX carries real interest rate and execution risk on Pratt and Whitney's engine issues. The regulatory and export control matters could recur: RTX was fined $200 million in 2024 for export compliance violations. The sensitivity grid above is there to make that uncertainty explicit rather than implying a single confident price target.

What would change my mind

The range rests on a 6.15% WACC, and the audit showed how far a wrong discount rate can move it: correcting a 1.22% equity risk premium took the DCF from $175 to $128 per share. A defensible WACC materially different from 6.15% would move the range again. At a 7.00% WACC with the same 3.0% terminal growth, the grid above gives $94.78 per share, well below the market price on the valuation date.
