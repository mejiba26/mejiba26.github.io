Project 01

RTX Corporation Equity Valuation

Fall 2024, revised 2026

Context

Independent equity research on a large cap defense contractor, built to answer one question: what is RTX actually worth per share?

My role

Built a DCF at a 6.15% WACC with a sensitivity grid across WACC and terminal growth, then cross checked it against peer P/E and EV/EBITDA multiples (Lockheed Martin, General Dynamics, Northrop Grumman). Benchmarked RTX against Boeing and Lockheed Martin on profitability, liquidity, and leverage. RTX's 19.21% gross margin was the strongest in the peer set.

Outcome

Fair value of $125 to $133 per share (DCF about $128, adjusted P/E about $125, EV/EBITDA about $133) vs. $122 at the time of analysis, so modestly undervalued.

What changed

Models built to be questioned, including my own. I used Claude to audit my original 2024 model and rebuilt it. The two biggest fixes: my discount rate rested on a 1.22% equity risk premium, and I subtracted total liabilities instead of net debt. My DCF estimate dropped from $175 to $128 per share, and every change is documented in a new Revision Notes appendix.

Project 02

ECG Patient Matching Model

Wake Forest School of Medicine Build-A-Thon · Sept 2026 to present

Context

A Build-A-Thon hosted by the Center for Artificial Intelligence Research (CAIR). The task: score whether two Lead I ECGs belong to the same patient.

My role

Led the modeling for a three person team. Built a Siamese CNN and feature neural net ensemble in Python (TensorFlow/Keras), trained on 37,000+ ECGs from 10,000 MIMIC-IV patients plus St. Jude paired hospital and Apple Watch ECGs.

Project 03

iShares Multi-Asset Portfolio Model

Mar 2008 to Aug 2026, out of sample

Context

A Python walk forward backtest of five allocation strategies (60/40, equal weight, inverse volatility, minimum variance, and max Sharpe with a 40% cap) across nine iShares ETFs: IVV, IJR, EFA, EEM, AGG, TLT, TIP, LQD, IAU.

My role

Each month, weights use only the prior 36 months of data. Costs are 10 bps on turnover. Stress tests cover the 2008 crisis, the COVID crash, and the 2022 rate shock.

Outcome

After costs, the plain 60/40 beat every other strategy: 8.4% a year with a 0.72 Sharpe, vs. 7.3% and 0.64 for Max Sharpe, the best of the rest. The optimizers earned their keep in crashes. In the 2008 crisis, Min Variance lost 1.2% and Max Sharpe 3.9%, vs. 29.6% for 60/40. But in the 2022 rate shock, when stocks and bonds fell together, every strategy lost between 13.9% and 15.9%, so diversification didn't help. Max Sharpe also traded heavily, with annual turnover of about 244% (buys plus sells).

Fed rate policy & equity valuations

Rate changes flow straight into the discount rate in every DCF I build. A higher rate, all else equal, lowers what a company is worth before the stock even moves.

Earnings season: valuation checkpoint & trading catalyst

Coca-Cola's Q3 (reported October 27) grew organic revenue 7%. Unit case volume rose 5% while price/mix added 1%, so volume did most of the work, though concentrate sales ran 2 points ahead of volume on shipment timing. Comparable EPS rose 11% to $0.91, and management raised full year guidance to at least 5% organic revenue growth and at least 11% comparable EPS growth. What it told me: growth that comes from people buying more holds up better than growth from raising prices, so it deserves more weight when I project revenue. On the trading side, earnings prints are some of the highest volatility moments I watch in NQ and ES. Research and trading meet at the same moment: the numbers update my fundamental view, and the reaction is what I'm positioned for.

Live simulation

Paper trading, aerospace and defense and oil

Started · As of

Simulation only. No real money, no broker, not investment advice.

The rules in plain language

  • Universe. RTX, LMT, NOC, GD and ITA (aerospace and defense); XLE, USO and XOM (oil). Cash earns nothing. Long only, no leverage.
  • Entry. Checked after each close. All four must hold: the close is above both its 50 day and 200 day average; the close is at least 1.5 times the 14 day ATR below the highest high of the last 10 trading days (ATR, average true range, is the typical daily price swing); today's close is above yesterday's high; and the portfolio has an open slot and has made fewer than 2 entries this week. The buy fills at the next day's open plus a 10 basis point cost.
  • Exit. The first of: the close falls to entry minus 2 ATR (stop); the close reaches entry plus 3 ATR (target); the close drops below the 50 day average (trend break); or 20 trading days pass (time stop). The sale fills at the next day's open with the same cost.
  • Sizing. Dollars at risk (the tier's risk per trade times equity) divided by the stop distance gives the shares, capped at the tier's maximum weight.
  • Tiers. Safe risks 0.5% per trade, max 20% in one name, 3 positions, 50% invested. Medium: 1%, 30%, 4 positions, 80%. High: 2%, 40%, 3 positions, 100%. Each starts with $100,000 of hypothetical money.

Honest limits

  • Swing trades in a small universe are infrequent. It will take months to collect enough trades to judge anything, and early results will be noisy.
  • Stops are checked on the close and filled at the next open, so a gap can lose more than the planned risk, and a trend break exit has no loss floor. Compare planned risk with the realized worst loss above.
  • A pullback can be the start of a bigger decline. The confirmation rule and the stop reduce that risk but do not remove it.
  • Fills at the open ignore slippage, and USO carries futures roll cost. Paper results do not predict real results.

Forecast evaluation

Volatility forecast scorecard

Started · As of

Forecast evaluation only, not trading advice.

What this shows

Every weekday after the market closes, four models each predict how wide the next day's trading range will be for four funds, meaning the gap between the day's high and its low as a percent. Each prediction comes with a band the model expects to hold the actual range about 8 times out of 10. The forecasts are saved to GitHub with a timestamp before the next session opens, then graded against what happened. Two questions are being tested: are the bands honest, so that they really hold about 80% of the time, and does a more elaborate model predict better than a very simple one. Nothing here says what to buy or sell.

How it works in plain language

  • What is forecast. The next trading day's log range, ln(High / Low), for SPY, QQQ, ITA and XLE. For values this small that is almost exactly the high to low gap as a percent, which is how it is shown here.
  • The four models. Naive: tomorrow looks like today. Rolling mean: the average of the last 22 days. EWMA: a weighted average that leans on recent days (lambda 0.94). HAR: a regression on yesterday, the last 5 days and the last 22 days, refit every Monday on all history up to the prior close.
  • The band. Each model's own errors over the previous 500 days set its 10th, 50th and 90th percentile forecasts. That makes an 80% interval built only from what was known when the forecast was made.
  • Grading. Mean absolute error is the average miss. Pinball loss scores the edges of the band. Coverage is how often the actual range landed inside the band, and it should sit near 80%.
  • Timing. Every forecast carries the UTC time it was generated. One generated after the next session's open is marked late, kept in the file and never scored. Nothing before the start date counts.
  • Fixed in advance. The models, their parameters and the test were written down before the first forecast. No ranking before 60 graded days. After 250, HAR is tested against the better simple baseline with a Diebold Mariano test, it only counts as better with a p value below 0.0125, and the result is published either way.

Honest limits

  • The daily range is an imperfect stand in for risk. It ignores overnight gaps and says nothing about direction.
  • Four ETFs that tend to move together are not four independent tests, so agreement across them is weaker evidence than it looks.
  • Every model here learns from past ranges, which say little about a sudden change of regime. Expect the bands to be too narrow right after a shock.
  • Shortened holiday sessions are graded like any other day, and the data is Yahoo Finance daily bars as first published.
